About the studio

A co-op built around the wheel

Earth'n Vessel is a local pottery co-operative in downtown Bay Shore. Our purpose is simple: to promote the art of pottery in our community.

Students start in a class, learn the basics of working with clay, and many stay on. Open studio time lets them keep throwing, trimming and glazing between sessions. Over the years the studio has become a place where people come for pottery and leave with friends.

Walk in the front door and you're in our gallery. Everything on the shelves was made by studio members. Walk a little further and you'll see pots being made, which is the best part of any visit.

Classes are led by Lisa, whose students describe her teaching as engaging and patient with first-timers.

We have a simple goal: a peaceful, supportive place where you can build real skill and find the artist in you.

How it works

From first lump of clay to finished pot

Step 1

Take a class

Learn wedging, centering and pulling up walls with an instructor beside you.

Step 2

Join open studio

Use studio time to practice. Repetition is how pots get thinner and lighter.

Step 3

Glaze and fire

Choose from the studio's glazes. Pieces go through a bisque firing and a glaze firing before they're ready to use.

Reasons people start

What you get from an hour at the wheel

Your phone stays in your pocket

Wet clay on both hands makes scrolling impossible. Centering takes your full attention, and that's the point.

You make things you'll use

The mug you drink from every morning can be one you threw yourself.

Progress you can hold

Your first bowl and your tenth sit side by side on the shelf. Improvement is something you can see.

A room full of people to learn from

In a co-op, the potter at the next wheel has made the same mistakes you're making and is happy to help.

It's forgiving

Until it's fired, clay can always go back in the bucket. Collapsing a pot is part of learning, not a failure.

It's been done for thousands of years

Pottery is one of the oldest crafts people have. You're joining a very long line.

First visit

Common questions

Do I need any experience?

No. Beginner classes assume you've never touched a wheel. You do need to be 16 or older.

How much does it cost?

$400 for a ten-week session. That includes glazes and firing for pieces 12" or under. Clay and a starter tool set are bought at the studio.

What should I wear?

Clothes that can get muddy, short or tied-back sleeves, and closed-toe shoes. Keep fingernails short if you can, and leave rings at home.

When do I get my pots back?

Pieces need to dry, be bisque fired, glazed and fired again, so plan on a few weeks from throwing to finished pot.

Can I bring a group or a friend?

Yes, people often sign up in pairs. For a larger group, send us a note on the contact page and we'll work out what fits.

How do I sign up?

Online booking is coming soon. For now, call the studio at (631) 665-0060 or send a message and we'll tell you when the next session starts.
